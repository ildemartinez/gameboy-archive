The Game Boy DMG-01 is Nintendo’s original handheld gaming system, released in Japan in 1989 and later introduced worldwide. Its distinctive monochrome display, interchangeable cartridges, simple controls, and remarkably efficient hardware established the foundation for the Game Boy family.

This section provides a general overview of the DMG-01, covering its history, physical characteristics, hardware architecture, regional variants, revisions, and major identifying features.

The DMG-01 evolved throughout its production life, with changes to its motherboard, display, components, plastics, labels, connectors, and internal construction. Although these revisions can appear subtle from the outside, they are important when identifying, documenting, repairing, or restoring individual units.

The following sections document the DMG-01 from both an external and internal perspective, treating the console as a physical artifact as well as a piece of hardware.

## Main features

The DMG-01 combines a reflective monochrome LCD, a custom 8-bit processor, four-channel audio, a directional pad, four action controls, and interchangeable cartridges. Its display shows 160 × 144 pixels using four shades selected through software-controlled palettes; the screen has no backlight. The power design uses four AA cells, with an external DC input also provided.

Its serial link port supports communication between compatible systems and peripherals. The port shape and protocol differ across later Game Boy models, so cross-generation connections may require the correct cable. The cartridge slot accepts original Game Boy software; later Color-exclusive cartridges are not supported.
